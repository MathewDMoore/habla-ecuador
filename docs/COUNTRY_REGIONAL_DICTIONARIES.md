# Country and regional dictionaries — build 201

Habla uses one evidence standard for every variety. Ecuador retains its AEL/CORPHA references; Bolivia, Mexico, Venezuela, Peru, Colombia and Cuba now have explicit national or regional research routes in the same source catalogue.

## Shipped scope

- 12 new reference records, for 26 named sources overall. The catalogue distinguishes a usable reference, a research lead and an access limitation.
- 24 new ASALE-attested senses, 19 expression/headword forms, with original U.S./U.K. English glosses and original bilingual examples. There are 111 regional dictionary cards in total; this does not count the separate Ecuador evidence collection.
- 23 of the new senses have an editorial figurative label. This is an overlapping semantic filter, not a replacement for form: an expression can be both an idiom and figurative. ASALE supports the senses; Habla supplies this browsing classification.
- Country references and coverage are visible in Dictionary on both entry pages. Counts follow the country/city or English counterpart selection, independently of search/tone filters. Missing national coverage is stated explicitly for the other available country views.
- Linked sources are references, not full imported dictionaries. No new record claims a national-dictionary cross-check, corpus query, frequency estimate, native-speaker review or city validation.
- No new phrase translation rule is installed. These are dictionary meanings and learning examples; context still determines translation.

## Entry evidence contract

Each new sense has a stable ID, exact headword/phrase, country labels for that sense, type, register, English variants, original examples, source ID/URL, exact source sense and checked date. Subnational labels retain their source code (for example Bo:E) and are shown separately from city evidence. The current new subregional senses are Eastern Bolivia; no city is inferred from that label.

Verification flags are independent:

1. dictionaryAttested: the actual sense and its geographic labels were inspected;
2. nationalDictionaryChecked: a named national source and sense must be recorded;
3. corpusChecked: an actual query, filters and concordance evidence must be recorded;
4. nativeSpeakerReviewed: record the review context and approved wording, not just the speaker’s nationality;
5. cityValidated: requires evidence for that city, not the publisher’s address or a country tag.

A source catalogue entry alone satisfies none of these entry-level checks. A book announcement or study abstract remains a research lead for its underlying lexicon.

## Phrase and sense boundaries

- dar bola: attention (Ecuador/Bolivia/Peru); acceptance of advances (Bolivia/Peru); free transport (Cuba). Each sense is separate.
- mamar gallo: teasing (Colombia/Venezuela) versus evasions/false promises (Colombia).
- por las huevas: unjustified reason (Ecuador/Peru) versus futile effort (Peru).
- tacho colorado and gallo’s unscrupulous-business sense retain Eastern Bolivia labels.
- Literal English learning aids and contrast notes are excluded from English counterpart search. A search for needle must not return Peru’s estar aguja as an English equivalent.
- Country inclusion is not exclusivity. ASALE may also attest countries outside this initial seven-variety pilot; no broader national parity is claimed.

## Expansion workflow

For each country, research ordinary vocabulary, idioms/fixed expressions, figurative senses, proverbs, collocations, register and false friends. Add a sense only after inspecting its actual source and geographic label. Corroborate through the national source, then corpus and local review as available; keep each uncompleted check false. Capture disagreement instead of silently merging labels or removing a competing sense.

Use short factual paraphrases and original examples. Review each publisher’s reuse terms before any bulk import. DEM’s CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 is recorded as a restriction; registering its URL is not a content license. Link to originals rather than copying a proprietary lexicon.

The new regional-sources suite checks all 24 records, source IDs, verification flags, seven-country coverage, separate senses, U.S./U.K. counterpart isolation, figurative/idiom overlap, subregion/city boundaries, source catalogue states and the cached source asset. Existing regional vocabulary preservation digests and translation/dictation/offline-retention regressions remain intact.
